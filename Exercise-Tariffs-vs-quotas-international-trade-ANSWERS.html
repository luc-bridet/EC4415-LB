B1: Unrestricted international trade. There is no tariff and no import quota.

Question 1  

Interpret the assumption of perfectly elastic world supply. Answer in no more than two sentences.

Answer

The country is small relative to the world market, so changes in its imports do not affect the world price. It can purchase any relevant quantity of sugar at the fixed price 49.

Question 2  

Construct the aggregate supply curve with unrestricted international trade.
Find the free trade (B1) equilibrium price, and the quantity produced, consumed, imported.
Find consumer and producer surplus.

Answer

Aggregate supply is the horizontal sum of domestic supply and world supply. Below the world price, the relevant supply is domestic supply. At the world price, imports are perfectly elastic, so aggregate supply becomes horizontal.

B2, B3, and B4: Import tariffs

We consider different tariff policies. A tariff is defined as tax paid upon importing 1 unit.

  • B2 is a tariff of 31 per unit
  • B3 is a tariff of 62 per unit
  • B4 is a tariff of 94 per unit
Question 3  

Construct the aggregate supply curve under B2, with a tariff of 31 per imported unit.

Answer

Foreign producers continue to receive the world price of 49. Since the government collects 31 per imported unit, domestic buyers pay 80 for imported sugar.

Import supply is shifted vertically by the tariff. Aggregate supply is then constructed as the horizontal sum of domestic supply and tariff-shifted import supply.

B5: Import licences granted for free

The government issues 1949 import licences. One licence is required to import one unit of sugar. The licences are granted for free.

Question 7  

Construct the importers' supply curve and the B5 aggregate supply.

Answer

Licensed importers buy sugar at the world price 49. Their supply is perfectly elastic at the world price up to 1949 units, then vertical because no further legal imports are possible.

Aggregate supply is the horizontal sum of domestic supply and licensed importers' supply.

Question 8  

Find the B5 equilibrium price, and the quantity produced, consumed, imported.
Find consumer and producer surplus, as well as the importers' surplus.

Answer

Importers buy at the world price and sell at the domestic price. Profit per licence is:

111 - 49 = 62.

Total profit is:

62 × 1949 = 121647.

B6: Import licences auctioned efficiently

The government auctions the same 1949 licences. The auction features many identical bidders, and each bidder bids exactly their willingness-to-pay.

Question 10  

What is an auction participant's willingness-to-pay for one import licence? Explain what changes between B5 and B6.

Answer

One licence allows its owner to buy one unit at the world price and sell it at the domestic price.

Willingness-to-pay per licence:

111 - 49 = 62.

Competition leads bidders to pay their full willingness-to-pay. Licence owners therefore receive net profit of 0.

Government auction revenue is:

62 × 1949 = 121647.

B7: Unexpected quota expansion

The government first auctions 1949 licences. It then surprises everyone by auctioning 1011 additional licences.

During the second auction, bidders know that the total number of licences will be 2960.

Question 11  

Find the B7 equilibrium price, and the quantity produced, consumed, imported.
Find consumer and producer surplus, as well as the importers' surplus.

Then find total government revenue from both rounds of licence auctions. You do not need to show your work.

Answer
Policy          P      Qp      Qc      Qi      PS      CS      GS      IS      TW
B7             80    1759    4719    2960   61563  575680  152994  -61236  729000

Revenue from the first auction remains: 121647.

After the quota expansion, willingness-to-pay for each new licence is:

80 - 49 = 31.

Revenue from the second auction is:

31 × 1011 = 31347.

Total government revenue is:

121647 + 31347 = 152994.
