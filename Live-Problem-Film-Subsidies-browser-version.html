FilmTaxation_B

In a fictional French-speaking country, Hollywood films and Domestically produced films are modelled as two separate and independent markets. Quantity is measured in millions of cinema tickets, and monetary surplus is measured in millions.

Consumers belong to either Group A or Group B. Each group has one demand curve in each market. Demand for one type of film is unaffected by the other type of film's market. Each market's aggregate demand demand is obtained by adding the two group demands. Each group's consumer surplus is the sum of its consumer surplus in the two markets.

The Hollywood-film competitive equilibrium is given by: price 144; total tickets 2055; producer surplus 0; Group A consumer surplus 34564; Group B consumer surplus 151899.

Question 1 [1.5]  

Show that the competitive equilibrium quantity in the Domestic-film market is 1404, and give the price and quantities purchased by each group.

Question 2 [1.0]  

The government imposes a tax of 35 per Hollywood ticket. Explain the incidence of the tax.

Question 3 [1.5]  

The government decides to use the tax revenue to subsidise each Domestic-film ticket by 51. A report commissioned by the Culture Minister projects tax revenue of 35 × 2055 = 71925 and subsidy expenditure of 51 × 1404 = 71604, and claims that the scheme will be balanced (and actually run a small net revenue). Discuss.

Question 4 [1.5]  

Call policy M the package of a tax of 35 per Hollywood ticket and a subsidy of 38 per Domestic ticket. Find the M equilibrium in each market. Show that the budget is balanced, up to rounding errors.

Question 5 [1.5]  

Find and interpret the deadweight loss from policy M across both markets.

Question 6 [1.5]  

Call policy S the package of a tax of 80 per Hollywood ticket and a subsidy of 62 per Domestic ticket. Find the S equilibrium in each market. Show that the budget is balanced, up to rounding errors.

Question 7 [2.0]  

Who benefits and who loses from the transition M → S? Quantify their stakes, including their collective willingness to pay to enact or block the transition. Explain.

Question 8 [1.5]  

On Twitter, @Mark and @Kris are arguing about the policy:

@Mark: Why exactly are we subsidising a bunch of Domestic films no one wants to see? And why are we punishing people who watch Hollywood films? The last Domestic film I saw was so bad, I haven't been to a cinema in months and I'm not planning to go back.

@Kris: Why are you complaining? You don't even watch films, so the tax does not affect you. And think of the 40,000 people who work in the film industry. This is their livelihood!

Critically assess Mark and Kris's discussion. Use no more than four concise bullet points.
